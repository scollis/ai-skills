# SWATS - Soil Water and Temperature System

SWATS provides vertical profiles of soil temperature, soil-water potential, and soil moisture as a function of depth below the ground surface at hourly intervals, deployed at the Central Facility and many extended facilities of the ARM SGP observatory.

## How it measures

The Model 229L Matric Potential Sensor operates on a heat-dissipation principle: a heat pulse is generated by supplying electrical current for a short time to a resistance heater inside the sensor, and heat is dissipated by water held in the soil pore spaces. The temperature rise resulting from the heat pulse is measured with a thermocouple junction inside the sensor; if pore spaces contain much water, a large amount of heat is dissipated and the temperature rise is small, while if pore spaces contain little water (mostly air), little heat is dissipated and a large temperature rise results. A calibration curve relates the temperature rise to soil-water (matric) potential. To obtain volumetric water content, the soil-water retention curve (unique for each soil, measured in the laboratory) relates water content to water potential, allowing an estimate of water content to be made from the potential value. Sensor measurements (initial temperature, temperature rise, potential, water content) are made and recorded at 1-hour intervals throughout the day.

**Siting.** Sensors installed at eight depths (5,15,25,35,60,85,125,175 cm) in two profiles 1 m apart per site (16 sensors total); rock or impermeable soil layers at some sites prohibited installation at greater depths; installation performed manually to minimize soil/vegetation disturbance; electronics enclosure mounted on a concrete slab above ground to minimize influence on sensors; soil texture, plowing history, vegetation density, water table depth, and proximity to roads/trees vary substantially by site and affect representativeness of measurements (detailed per-site notes given for each EF).

**Sampling.** native rate Sensor measurements made and recorded at 1-hour intervals throughout the day; all 16 sensors read within a 6-minute period; reported every hourly (dimension 'time' equals 24 per file) (hb p. 16).

## Quality control in this datastream

13 `qc_` companion variables cover 13 of the
33 data variables. Assessments present in the example file: `Bad`, `Indeterminate`.

`cleanup_qc=True` on read is what makes these usable; then screen with the `act-qc`
helpers. Remember that ARM uses two assessment vocabularies - `Bad`/`Indeterminate`
from the automated tests, `Incorrect`/`Suspect` after DQR normalisation - and that
filtering on only one of them silently keeps known-bad points.

```python
act_qc_table(ds)                       # what each bit would remove, per variable
act_qc_apply(ds, variables=[...])      # NaN-fill using all four assessment names
```

A single day is not a quality assessment of the instrument - it shows which tests
are live in this datastream and what a filter would do to the record.

Either way, check the DQRs before trusting a period - they carry the mentor's knowledge
of icing, misalignment and outages that no automated test catches:

```python
act.qc.print_dqr("sgpswatsE13.b1", "19960205", "20260923")
```

The handbook's own note on data quality: QC variables (e.g., qc_trise_W) are diagnostic variables equal to the sum of data quality flags for the corresponding variable, with flags: 0=within range, 1=missing (-9999), 2=below acceptable minimum, 4=above acceptable maximum, 8=failed delta check. To interpret, convert the QC value to base 2 to see which flags are set. QC variables exist for tref, tsoil, trise, soilwatpot, and soilwatcont. Instrument mentor inspects data from all sites at least once per week using graphical plots and data quality metric tables (DQ Explorer, NCVweb), and issues Data Quality Reports (DQRs) to the SGP Site...

## Known artifacts and failure modes

From the handbook. This is the section to read before concluding that a feature in
the data is atmospheric.

| issue | how it shows up | what the handbook says to do | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dry-end calibration uncertainty (300-2000 kPa range) | Large divergence between First- and Second-Generation calibration outputs for soil-water potential when soils dry beyond 300 kPa; no definitive data on sensor behavior in that range | No resolution at present; 'Third-Generation' calibration anticipated but not yet developed; users should be aware calibration choice matters more as... | (hb p. 17) |
| 229-L sensors insensitive to very wet conditions (0-10 kPa) | Sensor temperature rise does not change for potentials between 0 and about 10 kPa because ceramic wick does not begin to drain until suction reaches that level | Second-Generation calibration accounts for this behavior; First-Generation did not | (hb p. 17) |
| Sandy soils lack useful dynamic response range | In sand or loamy sand soils (EFs 4, 8, 15), sensors show either very wet or very dry readings with rapid transitions and no discernible drying trend; sensor wets more slowly/partially and... | Handbook states SWATS soil water data at EFs 4, 8, and 15 will never be representative of the true soil state | (hb p. 17) |
| Sensor failure/degradation over time | -9999 missing values or out-of-range/unreasonable readings in datastream; by 2002, 12 of 284 sensors giving bad readings and 2 dead; by 2015 many more sensors had failed or produced... | Mentor issues DQRs and works with Site Operations to fix problems via work orders | (hb p. 16) |
| Missing data flagged as -9999 | Value of -9999 in datastream indicates sensor depth not installed or data reporting missing | Check qc_ variables for flag=1 (missing) | (hb p. 10) |
| Delta check failures | QC flag=8 set when a value differs too greatly from the previous value (exceeds acceptable delta) | Flagged in qc_ variables; inspect flagged data before use | (hb p. 11) |
| Values outside acceptable min/max range | QC flag=2 (below acceptable minimum) or flag=4 (above acceptable maximum) set for tref, tsoil, trise, soilwatpot, soilwatcont | Use qc_ variables (sum of flags, decode in base 2) to identify affected records | (hb p. 11) |
| Lightning damage to instrument systems | Repeated data outages/damage at EF18 (Morris, OK) attributed to lightning strikes; one facility destroyed by lightning noted in FAQ (2002) | None specified beyond repair via maintenance/work orders | (hb p. 15) |
| Site-specific non-representativeness due to disturbed or unusual geology | e.g., EF24 Cyril soil moisture in a gypsum-filled hole not representative of surrounding wheat fields; EF11 Byron sensors at 125/175 cm installed under water table and site floods... | Noted in per-site user notes as caveats for interpretation | (hb p. 14) |
| Precipitation-induced signal changes not to be mistaken for anomalies | After a precipitation event, sensor temperature rise drops (increased effective specific heat of wetter soil), soil-water potential becomes less negative, and volumetric water content rises... | Recognize as expected physical response, not sensor error | (hb p. 11) |
| System replaced by STAMP | All SWATS data streams end/decommissioned in 2016 (except EF13 continued to July 2017 for comparison); users working with more recent data must use STAMP system instead | Use STAMP (Soil Temperature and Moisture Profile) system data for post-2016 period | (hb p. 7) |
| Data logger/storage module overwrite risk | Data logger internal memory holds ~3 days before overwrite; storage module holds ~15 days before overwrite, so infrequent downloads risk data loss | Regular data retrieval needed to avoid overwrite | (hb p. 16) |


## Calibration and maintenance

|  |  |
|---|---|
| Calibration method | Two-step calibration: (1) matric potential from raw temperature-rise (delta T) measurement using a sensor calibration equation, adjusted to a 'reference' sensor via linear regression using two points (wet and dry) per sensor to remove sensor-to-sensor variability; (2) volumetric water content estimated from potential... (hb p. 16) |
| Traceability | Calibration developed by correlating 229L sensor temperature-rise readings against thermocouple psychrometer potential measurements in a soil container as it dried; soil retention curves measured by OSU from soil samples collected at each site. (hb p. 16) |
| Routine maintenance | Corrective maintenance generally entails reconfiguring wiring in the above-ground electronics box and/or replacing electronics components as deemed necessary during troubleshooting; documentation in draft form, contact mentor for details. Preventative Maintenance Procedure Summaries available for SGP SWATS. (hb p. 23) |


Calibration history matters for trend work: a step at a calibration date is an
instrument event, not a climate signal.
